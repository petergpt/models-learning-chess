Four levels of evidence

  1. Attempt: the model declares a change.
  2. Behavior: its recorded play changes.
  3. Transfer: fresh matched games improve.
  4. Retention: the improvement persists.

Learning here is adaptation of notes and decisions within a run. Model weights remain unchanged.

Elo is the headline measure

Use the existing rolling 50-rated-game Elo and its credible interval. After 100 rated games, compare the first complete 50-game estimate with the latest 50-game estimate; those windows do not overlap. Before then, show the current rating and label the change as an early sample. A difference of medians is not an uncertainty interval for the change.

Supporting evidence and uncertainty

Game score means wins plus half of draws. Compare equal halves of complete six-game rotations, preserving opponent and color mix. Score-change intervals resample whole blocks 10,000 times, with at least five rotations per window for a verdict. These intervals are exploratory. Elo shading uses the existing 95% Bayesian credible interval. Plotting begins after four rated games; shading is clipped to the visible 1000–2000 range.

Test each strategy on fresh games

A declaration must appear in a snapshot saved before a game. Compare up to two complete rotations before and after the change, then the next two for retention. Treat the result as an association: several methods can change together. Saved-position drills and favorable custom positions cannot establish transfer.

Observe play without an engine

Replay recorded legal moves. Track illegal submissions, opening choices, castling by move 10, and non-pawn captures with at least a two-point material deficit over the learner move, enemy capture and next learner reply. Exchanges recovered within that horizon are excluded. Sacrifices may still count; these events are not certified blunders.

Conversion and efficiency

Conversion means winning a game after reaching at least +3 in standard material values after move 10. This ignores positional compensation and king safety. Submission times include pauses and are not compute time. Tokens have differing reported scopes and are not compared as an efficiency ranking.

Explain recurring failures

Keep authored diagnoses separate from mechanical observations. Label explanations as hypotheses and attach game references. Repetition of a defender, blocker or king-flight error can weaken a strategy claim even when the weaker opponent later loses. A filled checklist is not proof of reliable execution.

Keep the comparison independent

This operator report reads ledgers, saved notes and rating caches. It verifies hash chains, accepted-move legality, board states, results and fixed benchmark settings. It supplies no moves, changes no learner files and reads no private reasoning transcripts. Future causal tests need repeated runs, a fixed decision budget and controlled strategy changes.
