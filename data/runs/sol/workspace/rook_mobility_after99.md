# Own rook mobility study after benchmark 99

This is my manual reading of my finished game, not engine analysis or a forced-result proof.

After 32 Rh5 h6, the rook's downward h4 square was still empty. My actual 33 h4 occupied it. Later g4-g5 occupied the leftward square as well. A pawn can protect one rook destination while physically removing another escape; guards and mobility are different checks.

At 37...Bf7, Rh5 had no clean retreat: h4 and g5 were my own blockers, h6 was an enemy pawn guarded by g7, and Bf7 attacked h5 through g6. 38 gxh6 did not answer that bishop capture by force. Black played Kg8 rather than Bxh5, allowing 39 Rg5 after g5 had vacated and the h6 pawn had been removed. The recorded win therefore cannot be evidence of a safe rook trap.

40 Rxg7+ was protected by my pawn on h6. 40...Kxh6 removed that pawn, so the same rook on g7 was now attacked by the adjacent king. 41 Rg3 was legal along clear g6/g5/g4 and had the f2 pawn's guard. It also guarded Bf3 horizontally. Do not carry a captured pawn's guard into the next returned position.

Local alternative to test: after 32...h6, 33 Rh4 has an empty destination and preserves the h3 pawn below it. Its safety must be checked against the actual opponent reply. This is not a recommendation from a tool or an established defense.

Candidate check: identify my own blockers before advancing a pawn beside a rook, trace at least one actual retreat, and check the cheapest enemy attacker of that retreat. Do this from the real board; long invented continuations repeatedly failed to catch the actual trap.
