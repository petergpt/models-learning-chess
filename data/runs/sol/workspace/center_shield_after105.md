# Own study after benchmark 105

12 e4 Bb7 13 Be3 put my bishop between Qe2 and e4. Nc3 still attacked e4, but Black's b7 bishop guarded the knight after Nxe4; an immediate Nxe4 would not have the supposed queen recapture. Qd3 repaired that guard locally after the pawn had already been lost. Ndf6 then added another defender, and no pawn recovery was forced.

Own alternative from after 12...Bb7: 13 d5. The pawn on d5 would block the b7-e4 ray and have actual guards from e4, Bc4 and Nc3. The queen would remain on e2 with e3 empty. This is my unverified calculation, not proof of equality or a solved opening. Central exchanges can remove those guards and must be read from the actual board.

Later, 22 Be5 Bxe5 did not permit automatic dxe5: moving d4 would expose Qd3 to Rd8. Qe4 moved the queen to a pawn-guarded square instead. Actual Rxd4/cxd4/Qxe4/fxe4 left both own rooks alive and the queens gone. Black still had an extra bishop and two pawns for my extra rook; material was nominally equal there.

Black subsequently declined the e6 recapture, allowed exf7, left Bd3 for Bxd3, and left Rd1 for Rxd1. My bishop survived Bxd3; it was a whole enemy bishop capture, not an immediate fair bishop exchange. Both own rooks survived Rxd1. Those gifts matter to the win. The final promotion was guarded by Rd8, and Qh8 mate was referee-confirmed. This does not establish stronger-opponent transfer.
