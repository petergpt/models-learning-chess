# Own cheapest-capture study after benchmark 100

Read my finished PGN manually. No engine evaluation or automated analysis.

33...Rf3+ failed even though Bc6 guarded f3. The white g2 pawn could capture a five-point rook. Taking that pawn back with a bishop would not restore the rook or make the exchange even. My lengthy king-flight analysis missed the cheaper attacker already on the board.

After gxf3, immediately Bxf3 would place the bishop next to Kf2 without a real own guard of f3. I instead played Qh4+, received Kg1, then Qxh3. Only that actual queen on h3 supplied the direct horizontal guard used by 36...Bxf3. Keep these different returned positions separate.

The final Qg2 mate worked because Bf3 guarded the queen on g2, and the queen controlled f1/h1/f2/h2 while checking Kg1. This supported royal-adjacent check is different from an unguarded queen or rook check the king can capture.

Counterfactual drill to test: after 33 Kf2, Qh4+ rather than Rf3+. The e3 rook would remain under d4's pawn guard. Read the actual response before choosing another move; do not assert a continuation or saved whole game in advance.

For a checking move, inspect enemy pawns as well as the king. A real guardian prevents a whole-piece loss only when the accepted capture chain is favorable and its final recapture is legal. Merely listing a guardian is insufficient.
