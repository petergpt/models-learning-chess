The actual ending contained two failures that a guard count alone did not answer. After 61...Be3 62 Kf3, my bishop on e3 and pawn on f4 guarded each other. The bishop's other diagonal ran d4/c5/b6/a7. White's rook was b5. My king was c8, White's pawn a6, and my other pawn h6. Keep those actual bodies rather than an imagined rook placement.

62...Kd7 a7 63...Bxa7 removed the advancing pawn, but 64 Rb7+ checked my king on the seventh rank and attacked the bishop on a7. I could not save that bishop while answering the check; 64...Ke6 65 Rxa7 captured it whole. The nominal cost of this actual branch was bishop three for pawn one. I had expected White to take f4 instead and had missed the checking fork. Capturing an unguarded pawn did not make my capture safe from the next rook check.

Own untested alternative: 62...Kd8. The destination d8 is empty, not on the rook's b-file or fifth rank, not attacked by the a6 pawn, and distant from White's king f3. If the actual a7/Bxa7/Rb7 branch recurs, a rook on b7 does not check a king on d8. I would then have a bishop turn available, with Be3's f4 pawn guard as one candidate. This is a remembered-position hypothesis, not proof of a draw or a best move. The opponent can choose another reply.

The final ending supplied another missed checking capture. After 68...Kh4 69 Rf5, my king blocked my h5 pawn's forward square h4, and White's king on f3 blocked f4-f3. My move 69...Kh3 abandoned the h5 guard and allowed 70 Rxh5#. The rook on h5 checked down the h-file. White Kf3 controlled g2, g3 and g4; the checking rook controlled h2 and h4. None of those actual adjacent flights worked. This was a confirmed mate, not just another lost pawn. A prospective enemy capture must be tested for check and actual flights before treating it as an ordinary material loss.

Moving Ke6 first made the later Bd4 legal. King and pawn bodies can be route blockers or royal shields, even when all the intended endpoint squares look harmless.
