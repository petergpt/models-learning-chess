# Practice trial 03: audit the selected destination

Plan recorded on 2026-10-01 after game 42 and before game 43. This is practice, separate from measured games.

Target: prevent an immediate capture from being omitted while calculating a remote continuation. Game 41's Rd6/cxd6 is the clearest example. Game 42 also shows why a nearby king cannot automatically be counted as a recapturer and why the intended rook route must be physically clear.

Method: six compact exercises, approximately 10–20 minutes and at most 25 minutes from this plan. Two are remembered positions and four are self-composed contrasts using known mechanisms. This is deliberate practice, not a blind accuracy test. Before checking anything mechanically, identify the chosen landing square, both enemy pawn origins for a capture onto it, their actual occupants, other immediate captures in the supplied line, and the legal final recapture. Stop at that short concrete result; do not search remote variations. The checker may render boards and check only explicitly supplied moves, resulting occupancy and check status. No move menu, search, scoring, engine analysis or delegated advice.

Execution change in measured games: after selecting a rook/queen move or a capture, perform this small audit on the actual current board immediately before submitting it. Name the pawn origins even when empty. If a king is the intended recapturer, explicitly check the opponent's support of that square. If the audit changes the move, repeat it for the replacement. Use a concise internal factual record, not more general checklist items or claims that checking guarantees safety. For other moves, retain the ordinary checks/captures review.

Evaluation: games 43–48 under the unchanged paired-color rotation. Baseline is 1800 [1630, 1970], with 28 rated games. ## Manual answers, before mechanical verification

1. After Rd6, the black pawn origins for capturing d6 are c7 and e7. c7 contains a black pawn; e7 is empty. ...cxd6 removes White's rook and leaves that pawn on d6. Qxe3 then removes Black's bishop and leaves White's queen on e3. Relevant final inventory: White Kh1/Qe3/Rb1/Be5 and six pawns; Black Kg8/Qd5/Rf8 and four pawns, including the pawn now on d6. A rook has been exchanged for a bishop in this supplied line. The prior bishop protection of d6 did not prevent the pawn capture.

2. With c7 removed, both c7 and e7 are empty. There is no pawn capture onto d6 in that position. In the explicitly supplied alternative, ...Qxd6 removes Rd6, then Be5xd6 removes that queen. Final relevant major pieces are White Qe2/Rb1 against Black Rf8; White's bishop is on d6 and Black's bishop remains on e3. This counts a queen for a rook in that line. It does not show that Black must choose this exchange or that Rd6 is best.

3. Rxg6 removes the black g6-pawn and gives check along g6-g7-g8. The two black capture origins are f7 and h7, both occupied by black pawns. Each supplied reply, ...fxg6 or ...hxg6, removes the checking rook legally. The capturing pawn ends on g6; the other pawn stays on its seventh-rank square. White is left with Kg1 only. Giving check has not prevented the lower-value capture.

4. After ...Rxg3+, the relevant White pawn origins are f2 and h2, not f4/h4. Both contain white pawns. Either fxg3 or hxg3 removes Black's checking rook and leaves a white pawn on g3. Black is left with Kg8 only; White retains its king and two pawns. This is the colour-reversed rook-for-pawn loss.

5. ...Re6 lands on e6, whose White pawn origins d5 and f5 are empty. Rf7 does not change the black king's e4 location. The intended ...Re2+ cannot pass through that king on the e6-e5-e4-e3-e2 file. In the separate continuation, ...Re5 lands on e5; d4 and f4 contain no white pawn. Rf7xf3 then travels through f6, f5 and f4 and removes the black f3-pawn. ...Ke4xf3 would put the black king next to White's Kf2 and is illegal. With ...Rf5 instead, the black rook stands on f5, supported by Ke4, and pins Rf3 to Kf2. The supplied Rxf5/Kxf5 removes both rooks; the king capture is legal because f5 is not adjacent to Kf2 and neither remaining white pawn attacks it. Final inventory: Black Kf5/Pb6/Pg6/Ph5; White Kf2/Pb5/Ph4. This inventory is established separately from any claim that every alternative defense has been solved.

6. Replacing White's Kf2 with Kh2 changes the final capture's legality. After ...Re5/Rxf3, Kh2 attacks g1, g2, g3, h1 and h3; f3 is not among them. White's b5 and h4 pawns also do not attack f3. ...Kxf3 is now legal. Black retains Ke4-to-f3, Re5 and three pawns; White has Kh2 and two pawns. The same nearby black king can be a valid recapturer in this contrast and an invalid one in exercise 5.

All of these are my own known-example assessments. The supplied-move checker will verify only legality, occupancy and check status.
