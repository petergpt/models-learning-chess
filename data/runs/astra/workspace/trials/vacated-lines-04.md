## Manual reconstruction before mechanical checking

1. Remembered game 48 before 15...exd4: Black Kg8/Qd6; White Bg3. The e5 pawn blocks Bg3-f4-e5-d6. ...exd4 leaves e5 empty and a black pawn on d4, exposing Qd6. The supplied Bxd6 is legal and removes the queen; ...Rxd6 is legal and removes that bishop. All three resulting side-to-move check statuses are false. The branch exchanges Black's queen for White's bishop while also capturing the d4 pawn. It does not establish any other move as best.
2. Self-composed contrast: move the black queen from d6 to b6 in that starting board. ...exd4 still opens the g3-d6 diagonal, but no queen now stands on d6. The supplied g3-d6 is a legal quiet bishop move, followed by legal ...Rxd6. Black's queen remains on b6 and White's bishop is lost. None of the resulting positions checks the side to move. Identical move coordinates need not remove identical pieces.
3. Remembered game 47 after 40.Re3, before ...Nf3: Black Qd5 and Nd4, White Kd2/Qh2/Re3. Moving Nd4-f3 both attacks d2 with the knight and opens Qd5-d4-d3-d2. ...Nf3 is legal and checks White. Rxf3 is illegal because the queen's check remains. The rejected move leaves the same checked board.
4. Self-composed contrast to 3: add a white pawn on d3. After ...Nf3, that pawn stops the queen ray before Kd2. Rxf3 is now a legal capture of the sole checker; it does not check Black. This verifies only a changed legality condition, not a sound material choice.
5. Self-composed rook skewer using a remembered mechanism: White Kg1/Rc8; Black Kf8/Qh8; Black to move in check. ...Ke7 is legal. Clearing f8 opens Rc8-d8-e8-f8-g8-h8, and Rxh8 legally removes the queen. Neither resulting side-to-move position is in check. A legal king escape can expose a more valuable piece.
6. Self-composed contrast to 5: add a black bishop on g8. ...Ke7 is legal, but c8-h8 is now illegal because Bg8 remains in the rook's path. In a separate supplied branch, Rxg8 captures that bishop legally, and Qxg8 legally captures the rook and checks Kg1 along the g-file. The queen survives and Black trades a bishop for a rook in that branch. The origin was cleared, but another blocker remained.
7. Self-composed colour reversal: White Kg1/Qd3/Rd1/Pe4; Black Kg8/Bg6/Pd5; White to move. Both exd5 and the separate quiet e5 clear the e4 blocker on Bg6-f5-e4-d3. In either branch, ...Bxd3 removes White's queen, and Rxd3 then removes the bishop. All supplied moves are legal and none gives check. The quiet pawn move is as relevant to the opened line as the capture; Black's d5 pawn survives only in the quiet branch.
8. Self-composed knight blocker: White Kg1/Qf2/Re1/Nd4; Black Kg8/Bb6. Nf5 is legal and does not check Black, but clears Bb6-c5-d4-e3-f2. ...Bxf2 is legal, captures the queen, and checks Kg1. Kxf2 then legally removes the bishop: Black's king is distant and no other black piece supports f2. Black is not checked afterward. The legal king recapture does not recover the queen's value.
