# Own checking-knight-fork drills

These positions and candidates are my own. No engine analysis, automated search or delegated advice used. Both sessions will be explicitly stopped after at most four own moves and excluded from benchmark Elo.

Remembered case: benchmark 15 after ply 62, White against skill 3. My candidate is Re4-e3. In the recorded game, Re4-f4 permitted Nc3-e2+, which checked Kg1 and attacked Rf4. The f2/f3 pawns prevented the f1 rook from recapturing on f4. Re3 is intended to move the rook off both immediate checking-fork targets and put it on a square defended by Nc4 and f2. Pawn safety and a complete defense remain unproved.

Constructed case, White against skill 0:

`k7/8/8/8/4R3/3n4/6P1/7K w - - 0 1`

White: Kh1, Re4, Pg2. Black: Ka8, Nd3. My candidate is Re4-e3. The hand-identified threat is Nd3-f2+, checking Kh1 and attacking Re4 from f2. This changes the king, rook and knight locations from the remembered case, but remains a deliberately simple construction of a known motif. It is not independently validated as a best-move exercise or strength test. Observe the actual replies and whether a checking fork occurs; do not assume the planned check was played.
