**TRIAL 4 TABLE (block 5, games 25-30) — written in full, in this order, before any candidate is chosen; no row may be skipped, and 'obvious' moves (recaptures, forced king moves, retreats) are not exempt:**

- T2 LAST MOVE: the opponent's from-square and to-square; every rank, file and diagonal through the from-square with the pieces now at both ends; every square the moved piece attacks from the to-square, naming my pieces and pawns hit.
- T3 HANGING: for each of my pieces and pawns attacked, FIRST compare values: if any attacker is worth less than the attacked piece, the piece is lost unless it moves, the attacker is captured, or a piece interposes — write only those three options and never a defender count (game 26: 12...Qb6?? - T4 CHECKS: every check the opponent can give now (queen in all eight directions, each rook line, BOTH diagonals of each bishop, knight hops, pawn checks) with my king's flight squares and the attackers of each flight square; then the same list for the position after my candidate. - T5 CANDIDATE (at most two): the move's path square by square with occupants (sliders; knight (1,2)/(2,1); king destination lines); destination attackers and defenders listed PIECE BY PIECE over every enemy piece (each rook: its file and rank through the square; each bishop: both diagonals; the queen: all eight lines; knights; pawns; king) — a piece I attack along a line attacks me along the same line (game 27: 32.Qe7?? on the open e-file facing the rook on e2); the squares my move vacates and the lines opened through them for BOTH sides; each defence line I rely on, written with its intermediate squares and re-checked for my own pieces standing on it (game 24: 13...Nc6?? blocked c7-c6-c5).
- T5b ROOK TRAP CHECK (game 25): before any pawn move or capture that opens a square adjacent to one of my undeveloped rooks or queen, write that piece's escape squares and name every enemy bishop or rook line covering them (9.b3? Always read the FEN first (unambiguous), then cross-check with the diagram. Ranks 8..1 top to bottom, files a..h left to right.

1. **Orient**: side to move, castling rights, en-passant square, material count (list both sides' pieces by square).
2. **Opponent's last move**: what does it attack now? **Also: which enemy rook/bishop/queen lines pass through the square it just vacated?** A quiet retreat can be a discovered attack (game 6: 18.Bd3 unmasked Rc1 against my Qc7 and I lost the queen). 4. **Candidates** (2-4). For each candidate, play it in my head and ask:

   - **LINE OPENING: which enemy long-range pieces had a line blocked by the piece I am moving?** After it leaves, what do they now attack? (game 3: 16.Nd6?? unblocked b7-f3 and lost a knight.)
   - Which of my pieces does it leave en prise (including the piece that moved, and the pieces it stopped defending or whose line it blocked)?
   - Opponent's checks after it? Captures after it? A fork/pin/skewer? Back-rank mate? Queen/knight forks on my king?
   - Does it allow a pawn fork or a discovered attack?
4a. **Queen-safety count**: if my queen is beyond the 4th rank or adjacent to enemy pieces, count its safe retreat squares after the candidate move; fewer than 2 means retreat it first (game 5: 14.cxd5? allowed ...Rh5 trapping the queen).
