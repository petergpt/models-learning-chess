## Course
- London setup; Black loosened its king with 4...f6, 7...g5, 11...Kf7. 12.c4 opened the position; 12...Nb4 forked queen and bishop, answered by 13.Qb3 (defending d3 along the third rank).
- 15.cxd5 g4?! (ignoring d5) 16.dxe6+ kept the extra pawn and bought the tempo my attacked knight needed. 18.Qe4 (triple purpose: defends e6, hits g4 and the undefended Ra8) and 19.Qxa8 won a rook.
- 19...Nc6 boxed my queen in (b8, b7, c6, c8 all covered). 20.d5 kicked the knight and freed b8; then 21.Qxa7+ (I had not noticed it was check — harmless), 22.Qxb6 and 23.Qxa5 collected a knight and two pawns.
- Conversion: 25.Bxf6, 26.Rfe1 (so that e7 becomes a real threat), 28.Qxd5 offering the queen trade, 29.Qg5+ exploiting the king's lack of squares to win the rook, 34.e7, 37.Qh8#.

## Scan record (Trial 2 rules)
- Every enemy check was tagged; none was dangerous in this game, so the C3 forced-reply rule was not stressed.
- Avoided: 29.Qg5+ with the rook able to hit the queen through g6 (checked that Bxg7 removes it first); stalemate check before 36.Qxd4 (Black kept Kg8/Kh8/Kh6/h4).
- Slip: 21.Qxa7 was a check I had not registered — a line-C omission on my own move, cost nothing.

## Assessment
The 1800 setting played far below its benchmark level here (unprovoked king walk, un-recaptured pawns). This is not evidence about block 3; the useful part was rehearsing the written scan with explicit check tags for 37 moves without an illegal attempt.
