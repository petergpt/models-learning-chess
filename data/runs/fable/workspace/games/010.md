## Course
- Opening/middlegame: good; won two pawns by move 26 (24...exf4, 25...fxe3) against an uncastled king and traded into a favourable ending.
- Errors: 45...g6?! and the earlier decision to leave the e7 rook where a knight could hit it; 46.Nxe7 cost me the exchange because I had the value arithmetic backwards ("N for R, +2 for me" — it is −2). After that I was down the exchange for a pawn and had to defend a long rook-vs-bishop ending with my bishop tied to the a-pawns.
- Saves: the scan caught 42...Ke5?? (Re4 would have been mate), 51...Kc7! (making Rxb6 impossible), 60...a6 (only playable with the king defending b6), the trap 64...Kb4/65...Kc5 repetition try, and 69...a3/70...a2! which forced White's king to b2.
- Decisive moment: 71...Be5+! — check on the b2 king along e5-b2 while the bishop also hit the f6 rook; White could not both escape the check and save the rook. 72...Bxf6, 73...a1=Q, mate on move 80 with careful stalemate checks.

## Lessons
- Exchange arithmetic must be written with piece values; "defended" is irrelevant when a cheaper piece attacks a dearer one (procedure v2.4 D-line patched during the game).
- Skill-0 does not understand king walks into the bishop's diagonals; keeping the bishop and looking for double attacks (check + attack) paid off.
- Repetition awareness: at −1 material I was happy to repeat; the engine did not.
