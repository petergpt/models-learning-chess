## Course

- 15...Ne7! took away g6 from my knight and prepared ...f6. After 15...Ne7 the Ne5 had NO retreat: c4 (b5 covers), c6 (b7), d3 (c4 pawn), d7/f7 (king), g4 (h5), g6 (Bh7, Ne7), f3 (my own pawn). I noticed only when scanning 16.e4 — one move too late. No move saved it (b3, g4, Bg5, Bg3 all lose it too); I chose 16.e4 (hoping for ...dxe4 freeing f3) and after 16...f6 gave it up with 17.Nd3 cxd3 18.Bxd3 keeping the structure.
- Scan discipline itself was fine: every move was written, no one-move oversight of checks/captures.

## Classification
New error class, not a one-move oversight: a PIECE-TRAP two moves deep (the opponent's quiet move removed the last retreat square, the next pawn move attacked the piece). My own 14.f3 and 15.Bf1 helped the trap (f3 blocked the natural retreat; the bishop on f1 did not cover g4/d3).

## Rule added (procedure v2.7)
- E. Mobility line for advanced pieces: for every piece of mine beyond my 4th rank (or any piece the opponent can attack with a pawn next move) list its safe squares AFTER the candidate move and after the opponent's most natural reply; a piece with zero safe squares and an available pawn attack is a hanging piece. Never block my own knight's retreat square with a pawn (14.f3) without first checking that another exit exists.
