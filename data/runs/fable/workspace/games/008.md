1. e4 c6 2. d4 d5 3. e5 Bf5 4. h4 h5 5. Bd3 Bxd3 6. Qxd3 e6 7. Nf3 Nd7 8. Nc3 Ne7 9. Bg5 Qb6 10. O-O Qxb2?! 11. Rfb1 Qa3 12. Nb5 Qxd3?? 13. Nd6+ Kd8 14. cxd3 Rh7 15. Rxb7 g6 16. Rc1 Nb8 17. Rcb1 Nd7 18. Rxd7+ Kxd7 19. Rb7+ Kd8 20. Bf6 — resigned (Ng5 next wins the h7 rook or mates on f7; my king had no flight squares: c8/e8 covered by Nd6, c7/d7 by Rb7, Ne7 pinned by the bishop).

## Errors
1. 10...Qxb2?! — submitted immediately after an interface nudge, no written scan (third such lapse). The pawn grab was playable in principle but needed an exact follow-up I had not checked.
2. 12...Qxd3?? — I "verified" the reply 13.Nd6+ Bxd6 without tracing the bishop's path: f8-e7 was blocked by MY OWN knight, so Bxd6 was illegal and 13.Nd6+ forced Kd8 (only legal move). Correct was 12...Qa5/Qa4/Qa6 (safe squares), since Qd3 and Nb5 both attacked a3 after the knight vacated c3 — that part the scan did catch (line B: opponent's vacated square).
3. Attempted 13...Bxd6 (illegal) — same path error, costing an illegal attempt.
4. From move 13 my king had zero flight squares. Everything after that was a mating net (pinned Ne7 on the f6/g5-d8 diagonal, Rb7 covering c7/d7, Nd6 covering c8/e8, f7 the mating square for Ng5xf7).

## Good
- The scan caught two self-destructive moves in analysis: 15...Nf5 (illegal — would expose my king on the g5-d8 diagonal) and 20...Bh6/Bg7 (allow Bxe7#). It also caught 12...cxb5?? (Qxa3) via line B.

## Rules added (procedure v2.4)
- Reply-move legality: when a scan line relies on MY reply (a recapture or block), trace that reply's path and pin status exactly like the submitted move.
- King-air rule: never allow a position where my king has zero flight squares while the opponent has any checking piece in range; if the current move leaves zero flight squares, it must be rejected unless forced.
- Line B explicitly includes my own king: "does the piece I move uncover a line from an enemy slider to MY king?" (pin check).
