# Game 2 — Black vs full-strength — 0-1 by checkmate (23.Nxf6#), 45 plies, 0 illegal attempts

Opening: Caro-Kann Advance 4.h4 h5 5.Bd3 Bxd3 6.Qxd3 e6 7.Nf3 Qb6 8.0-0 Qa6 9.Qd1 Ne7 10.Bg5 Nf5 11.Nbd2 c5 12.c4 dxc4 13.dxc5 Bxc5 14.Ne4 Be7 15.Bxe7 Kxe7 16.Qd2 Rhd8 17.Qg5+ Ke8 18.Qxh5 Nh6 19.Nfg5 Kf8? 20.Nh7+ Kg8 21.Nef6+! gxf6 22.Qxh6 Nc6?? 23.Nxf6#.

What went wrong (own assessment):
1. **19...Kf8?** — I rejected 19...Qb6/19...Qd3 because concrete lines cost a pawn (Nd6+ ideas) and chose the king move "to avoid checks". Against a strong attacker, king safety outranks a pawn. The king on f8/g8 with my rook h8 gone and no g/h pawns was a mating net.
2. **Knight-check blindness**: I analysed "21.Nhf6+ Kf8!" but the OTHER knight checked (21.Nef6+), and the h7 knight kept f8 covered, so gxf6 was forced. Rule: when two knights can reach a square, analyse each, and note what the remaining knight still controls.
3. **Queen relocation blindness**: after 22.Qxh6 the queen covered f8 through g7; my planned "...Kf8" was illegal, so 23.Nxf6 was mate, not a pawn grab. Rule: after any enemy queen/bishop move, recompute my king's flight squares along the new diagonals before trusting a defensive line.
4. Earlier: 15...Kxe7 (vs 15...Nxe7) kept the king central; probably acceptable, but the sequence 16...Rhd8 17.Qg5+ Ke8 18.Qxh5 lost the h-pawn and castling rights for little. 16...Nd7 or 16...Nc6 first was safer.

Good: the opening was fine and I won a pawn with 12...dxc4 after verifying 13.Nxc4? Qxc4.
Transferable error classes: (a) king-flight-square bookkeeping after each enemy piece relocation; (b) considering both knights for a knight check; (c) preferring material over king safety under attack.
