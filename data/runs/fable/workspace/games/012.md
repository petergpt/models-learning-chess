## Course
- White played a London setup; 5...Bxf4 gave White doubled f-pawns and 12...Nxe5 13.fxe5 Qxe5 won the e5 pawn. 18...e5/19...e4 took space, 25...Nxd4 traded queens a pawn up.
- 30.a6 bxa6 handed me a second pawn; 34.cxd5 ... 37...Kxd5 kept +2. 42...Rxb2 and 44...Rxf2 made it +3 with a passed e-pawn on e3; 45.Rxa7+ took one back.
- Moves 45-54: ten consecutive rook checks from White's two rooks (a perpetual attempt). I avoided repetition by choosing a new king square each time, used 50...Kb7 (attacking an undefended rook) to force 51.Rca3 and gain the tempo for 51...e2, then 54...Ke4 reached a square from which every White check lost a rook. 55...e1=Q, 56...Qxf2 gave queen and rook against rook.
- 57...Kf3?! threatened Qg2 mate but allowed 58.Rf4+: the forced king move off the f-file uncovered the rook's line onto my queen (59.Rxf2 Kxf2). Rook and three pawns against two pawns still won: 60...Re3+ 61.Kh2 fxg4 and 62...Rh3#.
- Illegal attempt: 35...Rec8 with my own Rd8 blocking the e8 rook's path (corrected to Rdc8).

## Scan record
- Caught: 45...Kb7?? (on the checking rook's rank — exactly the game-11 rule), 45...Kb8/Kc8 (Ra8+ would then win my Re8, blocked behind my own king), 48...Ke6 (would block my rook's guard of e3 and allow Rxe3+), the stalemate check before 60...Re3+ and 61...fxg4.
- Missed: 57...Kf3 — line C listed Rf4+ as "harmless, reply Ke2/Ke3" without treating the forced reply as a move of its own (line B for the reply: my king leaving f3 uncovers f4-f2). Not result-changing, but it is the same class as the decisive errors of games 11 and 6: a written scan line answered superficially.

## Classification
First win against the 1800 setting. No decisive one-move oversight; one non-decisive one (57...Kf3). Endgame technique against the two-rook perpetual was adequate (tempo-gaining king moves, repetition tracking).

## Rule added (procedure v2.6)
- C3. Forced-reply discovery: for every enemy check in line C, treat my required reply as a move and run line B on it — the square my king or blocker leaves may uncover an enemy line onto a piece behind it. If I stand between an enemy rook/queen line and my own piece, any check that forces me off that line is a discovered attack.
